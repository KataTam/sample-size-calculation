# Assessment rubric

The core submission is one concise calculation report developed through four activities: simulation, one chosen calculation, a shared-loss recruitment adjustment, and report review. A supplied case and brief explanations are sufficient. Score each applicable criterion 0 (missing or incorrect), 1 (partly justified) or 2 (clear, accurate and justified). Core criteria 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 and 10 give a maximum of 12. Advanced assignments add selected criteria 3, 6, 8 and 9; the maximum is 20 when all are assessed. The score should state the criteria used. Scores describe this educational task; they do not certify a real protocol as ready for use.

| Criterion | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| 1. Question and goal | Clear outcome/time point and a testing, estimation or fixed-resource aim aligned with the intended conclusion |
| 2. Differences and clinical importance | Roles of difference observed in previous data, target difference and clinical threshold are distinguished; equal values or different numerical orders are accepted when justified. Units, sign, patient relevance and consequences of the chosen target are clear |
| 3. Evidence appraisal: advanced | Sources or hypothetical status, population and time point are stated; limitations, conflicting findings where available, and plausible ranges inform the assumptions |
| 4. Analysis and calculation | Appropriate stated test/interval and assumptions; reproducible calculation with method limits acknowledged |
| 5. Sample and recruitment counts | Correct per-group and total rounding; dropout inflation distinguished from fixing missing-data bias |
| 6. Sensitivity scenarios: advanced | Plausible alternative effect, SD or risk evaluated at fixed planned n and interpreted |
| 7. One versus many studies | One result distinguished from conditional power and Type I error across hypothetical studies; no simulation required; non-significance is not proof of no effect |
| 8. Precision and clinical interpretation: advanced only | Estimate and interval interpreted against zero and the clinical threshold; for estimation or feasibility, useful precision and the correct denominator are justified |
| 9. Simulation uncertainty: advanced only | Participants and simulation repetitions distinguished; generating assumptions, seed and analysis recorded; Monte Carlo uncertainty and failed analyses addressed where relevant |
| 10. Feasibility and communication | Concise, reproducible report with justified supplied inputs and clear limits; advanced work adds a detailed resource and feasibility assessment |

For a short activity use criteria 1, 2, 7 and 10 (maximum 8), with prepared outputs if needed. Give criterion-level feedback and request revision of substantive misconceptions regardless of total score. Calibrate scoring between teachers using two sample responses before the pilot. Do not apply unvalidated pass/fail bands as evidence of clinical competence.

## Feedback prompts

- Which difference was observed in earlier data, which is the calculation target, and which is a judgment about importance?
- Which evidence supports the plausible range, and what finding would change your assumption?
- For advanced estimation: your interval excludes zero. Does it also exclude effects below the clinical threshold?
- What is fixed while you change the generating scenario?
- Would increasing replications alter the power of each study?
- What useful conclusion is still possible with the available resources?

## Evidence from the consolidated book

For advanced criterion 6, an alternative scenario should state whether sample size is recalculated or held fixed, with its consequences explained. For criterion 7, a correct explanation of conditional power and one inconclusive result is sufficient for the core route; repeated-study outputs are optional evidence. For advanced criterion 9, require the simulation batch size and Monte Carlo interval, distinguishing B from patients per group. Advanced precision work should distinguish anticipated width from a guarantee for every realized interval.

For a single-population estimate, adapt criterion 2 to the target quantity, units and useful precision rather than demanding three treatment differences. For a feasibility study, assess justified objectives and progression criteria. For an existing dataset, assess the fixed sample rationale, completeness and limits. Omit inapplicable testing criteria and state the adjusted maximum; do not penalize a legitimate estimation question for lacking an efficacy hypothesis.
