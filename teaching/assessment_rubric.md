# Assessment rubric

Assess a case worksheet and 300–600 word justification, with a saved simulation output or the equivalent prepared static results. Coding proficiency is not required. Score each criterion 0 (missing or incorrect), 1 (partly justified) or 2 (clear, accurate and justified). Maximum 20. Scores describe performance on this educational task; they do not certify a real protocol as ready for use.

| Criterion | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| Question and goal | Clear outcome/time point and a testing, estimation or fixed-resource aim aligned with the intended conclusion |
| Effects and clinical importance | Expected, planning and clinically important effects are distinguished; units, sign and patient relevance are clear |
| Assumption evidence | Sources or hypothetical status are stated; small pilot estimates are treated as uncertain |
| Analysis and calculation | Appropriate stated test/interval and assumptions; reproducible calculation with method limits acknowledged |
| Sample and recruitment counts | Correct per-group and total rounding; dropout inflation distinguished from fixing missing-data bias |
| Sensitivity | Plausible alternative effect, SD or risk evaluated at fixed planned n and interpreted |
| One versus many studies | One realisation distinguished from power/Type I error across replications; non-significance is not treated as proof of no effect |
| Precision and clinical interpretation | Full width, coverage and clinical threshold interpreted without confusing statistical significance and clinical importance |
| Simulation uncertainty | Participants and replications distinguished; seed/method recorded; Monte Carlo error separated from uncertain assumptions |
| Feasibility and communication | Informational value, participant burden, resources and limits support a coherent recommendation |

For a short activity use criteria 1, 2, 7, 8 and 10 (maximum 10), with prepared outputs if needed. Give criterion-level feedback and request revision of substantive misconceptions regardless of total score. Calibrate scoring between teachers using two sample responses before the pilot. Do not apply unvalidated pass/fail bands as evidence of clinical competence.

## Feedback prompts

- Which effect is an expectation, and which is a judgement about importance?
- Your interval excludes zero. Does it also exclude effects below the clinical threshold?
- What is fixed while you change the generating scenario?
- Would increasing replications alter the power of each study?
- What useful conclusion is still possible with the available resources?

## Evidence from the consolidated book

For criterion 6, accept the chapter 4 curve/table with a recruitment-cap interpretation and an alternative effect evaluated at fixed n. For criterion 7, accept chapter 2's rejection fractions and p-value histogram plus a correct null-scenario explanation. For criterion 9, require the chapter 5 batch size and Monte Carlo interval, distinguishing B from patients per group. For criterion 8, ask whether meeting an expected width target guarantees each study's interval width (it does not). Keep the ten criteria and 20-point maximum; these are examples of evidence, not extra marks.
