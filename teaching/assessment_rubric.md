# Assessment rubric

Assess a case worksheet or assumptions report and a 300–600 word justification. The core submission includes a calculation and sensitivity table or curve; it does not require simulation. Score each applicable criterion 0 (missing or incorrect), 1 (partly justified) or 2 (clear, accurate and justified). Core criteria 1–8 and 10 give a maximum of 18; the advanced simulation extension adds criterion 9 for a maximum of 20. Report the route and denominator with the score. Scores describe this educational task; they do not certify a real protocol as ready for use.

| Criterion | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| Question and goal | Clear outcome/time point and a testing, estimation or fixed-resource aim aligned with the intended conclusion |
| Differences and clinical importance | Expected difference, target difference and clinical threshold are distinguished; units, sign, patient relevance and consequences of the chosen target are clear |
| Assumption evidence | Sources or hypothetical status, population and time point are stated; uncertain pilot estimates and plausible ranges are acknowledged |
| Analysis and calculation | Appropriate stated test/interval and assumptions; reproducible calculation with method limits acknowledged |
| Sample and recruitment counts | Correct per-group and total rounding; dropout inflation distinguished from fixing missing-data bias |
| Sensitivity | Plausible alternative effect, SD or risk evaluated at fixed planned n and interpreted |
| One versus many studies | One result distinguished from conditional power and Type I error across hypothetical studies; no simulation required; non-significance is not proof of no effect |
| Precision and clinical interpretation | Estimate and interval interpreted against zero and the clinical threshold; for estimation or feasibility, useful precision and the correct denominator are justified |
| Simulation uncertainty: advanced only | Participants and simulation repetitions distinguished; generating assumptions, seed and analysis recorded; Monte Carlo uncertainty and failed analyses addressed where relevant |
| Feasibility and communication | Informational value, participant burden, resources and limits support a coherent recommendation |

For a short activity use criteria 1, 2, 7, 8 and 10 (maximum 10), with prepared outputs if needed. Give criterion-level feedback and request revision of substantive misconceptions regardless of total score. Calibrate scoring between teachers using two sample responses before the pilot. Do not apply unvalidated pass/fail bands as evidence of clinical competence.

## Feedback prompts

- Which effect is an expectation, and which is a judgment about importance?
- Your interval excludes zero. Does it also exclude effects below the clinical threshold?
- What is fixed while you change the generating scenario?
- Would increasing replications alter the power of each study?
- What useful conclusion is still possible with the available resources?

## Evidence from the consolidated book

For criterion 6, accept the chronic-pain sensitivity table or an equivalent calculation with the original sample held fixed. For criterion 7, a correct explanation of conditional power and one inconclusive result is sufficient for the core route; repeated-study outputs are optional evidence. For advanced criterion 9, require the simulation batch size and Monte Carlo interval, distinguishing B from patients per group. Advanced precision work should distinguish anticipated width from a guarantee for every realized interval.

For a single-population estimate, adapt criterion 2 to the target quantity, units and useful precision rather than demanding three treatment differences. For a feasibility study, assess justified objectives and progression criteria. For an existing dataset, assess the fixed sample rationale, completeness and limits. Omit inapplicable testing criteria and state the adjusted maximum; do not penalize a legitimate estimation question for lacking an efficacy hypothesis.
