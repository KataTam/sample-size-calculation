# Case: A pilot with limited information

All numerical values are hypothetical teaching assumptions.

A team developing a rehabilitation pathway has limited evidence about its effect. Three months of staff time and recruitment capacity permit 40 participants. The pilot asks whether recruitment, delivery, follow-up and outcome measurement are feasible. It does not aim to establish treatment efficacy.

Before planning, review relevant systematic reviews, previous studies, service records and patient/clinical expertise. State which information is transferable and uncertain. Define feasible delivery and useful follow-up precision, then agree progression criteria before results are seen.

The app scenario assesses a retention proportion at fixed total n = 40, anticipated retention 0.80 and 95% confidence. It compares realised Wilson intervals with a full-width reference of 0.20. The shared definition is `pilot_feasibility` in `R/teaching_cases.R`.

[Open the tutorial and app](https://katatam.github.io/sample-size-calculation/study/?activity=pilot_feasibility).

1. If 32 of 40 complete follow-up, interpret the retention estimate and interval.
2. Compare the interval with a prospectively agreed retention criterion. Explain why an 80% point estimate does not establish retention of at least 80%.
3. Consider whether a second site or longer period could give 80 observations, and whether the narrower interval is useful for the decision.
4. Identify what this pilot could inform about a future study and what it could not establish about treatment benefit.
5. Record resource limits, expected recruitment rate, reasons for missing outcomes and the planned main-study analysis.

See [the CONSORT randomized pilot/feasibility extension](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i5239) and the archived [NIHR HTA guidance example](https://njl-admin.nihr.ac.uk/document/download/2023130). A reporting guideline is not a universal minimum-n rule; check current funding-call requirements. Small pilot treatment effects remain uncertain and should be assessed alongside wider evidence and plausible ranges.
