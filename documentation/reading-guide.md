# Reading guide

Use the [full references](references.md) for bibliographic details and publisher links. Choose a source for the planning decision you need to make; reading every paper is not a prerequisite. Keep one study plan alongside the tutorial and use reading to improve its justification.

## Start here: the core study plan

| Decision | Suggested reading | Use it in the tutorial |
|---|---|---|
| Connect the question, outcome and analysis | Fong et al. (2026); Wang and Ji (2020); Zrineh et al. (2026) | Check the chronic-pain question before using a calculator; distinguish sampling quality from sample size |
| Choose and justify the target difference | [DELTA²: Cook et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.k3750) | Explain importance and realism, then compare the target with the expected difference and clinical threshold |
| Select a defensible planning goal | [Lakens (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267) | Explain whether testing, useful precision or fixed resources drives the plan |
| Assess prior evidence | Albers and Lakens (2018); Caldwell and Vigotsky (2020) | Treat small pilot differences as uncertain; use clinically interpretable units and plausible ranges |
| Report the calculation | [CONSORT 2025 explanation and elaboration](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-081124) | Record target justification, event rates or SD, error rates, method, counts and losses in the assumptions report |

Table: Reading that supports decisions in the core study plan.

Complete the [assumptions report](https://katatam.github.io/sample-size-calculation/study/?activity=assumptions_report) progressively. The chronic-pain sensitivity table makes the consequence of choosing a target larger than the clinical threshold visible. Reporting guidelines help document decisions; they do not establish the correct target or supply universal input values.

## Advanced methods and reference books

*Power Analysis with Superpower* and Lakens and Caldwell (2021) support exploring repeated studies and power curves; Pargent et al. (2024) provides a structured mixed-model simulation workflow. Use these with the one-study, power-curve and repeated-trial activities, preserving the clinical tutorial's stated analysis methods. Sandoval, DICE and Rudolph provide complementary teaching examples. More simulation repetitions improve Monte Carlo precision, not the proposed study's power.

Perugini et al. (2014) introduces safeguard power; Kunzmann et al. (2021) and Pezeshk (2003) discuss approaches that account for parameter uncertainty. These belong with a justified advanced objective, not as automatic sample-size reductions. Shamsutdinova et al. (2026) is a prediction-planning preprint and should be read as emerging work.

Julious (2023) is a reference for design-specific sample size methods; the earlier Julious (2004) review focuses on normal data. Piantadosi (2024) covers broader trial methodology, and Kirkwood and Sterne (2003) supplies general statistical background. Book descriptions and reading notes identify useful scope; consult the relevant chapters before adopting a formula. R documentation and Newcombe (1998) support implementation details in the methods appendix.

## Teaching, feedback and adaptation

Our account in Wieringa et al. (2025) informs learner and teacher involvement in development. Paskevicius, Paskevicius and Irvine, and Cronin and MacLaren support aligning learning outcomes, activities, assessment and opportunities to contribute. Orsini et al. (2024) motivates preparation and guided simulation discussion, while its reported workshop feedback is not evidence that this local module improves attainment.

Use the [teaching guide](../teaching/TEACHING_GUIDE.md), [alignment table](../teaching/constructive_alignment_table.md) and [assessment rubric](../teaching/assessment_rubric.md) together. Evaluate conceptual reasoning and transfer separately from satisfaction. The [adaptation guide](../teaching/adaptation_guide.md) explains how to keep source, app settings and assessment consistent; Bothmann et al.'s open-source educational-resource manuscript provides a useful development perspective.

See [evidence and design rationale](evidence-and-design-rationale.md) for how these sources informed the teaching design and what remains to be evaluated locally. External papers retain their own terms; use publisher or library access rather than expecting copies in this repository.

## Find a resource for your study

The [study-design guide](../student-materials/study-design-guide.md) distinguishes setting, timing, assignment, dependence and purpose. It links primary resources for trials, pilot feasibility, observational studies, diagnostic accuracy, prediction and preclinical experiments. CONSORT/SPIRIT, STROBE, STARD, TRIPOD+AI and ARRIVE are reporting resources; they do not replace a calculation matched to the planned analysis.

For pilot planning, begin with the [CONSORT randomized pilot/feasibility extension](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i5239) and [Albers and Lakens on uncertain pilot effects](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2017.09.004). The linked NIHR HTA document is an archived example; consult current funding-call requirements for an actual application.

For prerequisite reminders and common questions, use the [glossary](../student-materials/glossary.md) and [common mistakes](../student-materials/common-mistakes.md), also included in the tutorial. Formulas and formatted results remain visible; R code and raw output can be expanded together.

## Source coverage and access

The author's literature folder contains papers and separate reading notes. Some notes cover only an abstract, bibliographic record, book contents or selected passages; they are not substitutes for reading the full source before adopting a method. Verify unresolved manuscript metadata before a formal citation. Original e-learning materials provide historical provenance, while clinical guidance and methodological papers support current claims. Software acknowledgments are separate from statistical evidence.

Link to publishers, repositories or library access rather than placing the local paper collection in the website or source download. The current site and repository remain a private working edition.
