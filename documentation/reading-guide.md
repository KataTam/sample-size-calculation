# Reading guide

Use the [full references](references.md) for bibliographic details and publisher links. The reading collection supports three parts of the resource:

- Sample-size reasoning: Lakens on justifying sample size, and the clinical introductions cited in the tutorial.
- Simulation and interpretation: *Power Analysis with Superpower* inspired the repeated-study, power-curve and generate/analyze/repeat activities. The tutorial adapts these ideas to medical examples. The methods documentation explains the actual calculations used here.
- Teaching and open practice: Paskevicius, Paskevicius and Irvine, and Cronin and MacLaren inform the alignment of activities, assessment and opportunities to contribute.

See [evidence and design rationale](evidence-and-design-rationale.md) for how these sources informed the teaching design and what remains to be evaluated locally. External papers retain their own terms; use publisher or library access rather than expecting copies in this repository.

## Find a resource for your study

The [study-design guide](../student-materials/study-design-guide.md) distinguishes setting, timing, assignment, dependence and purpose. It links primary resources for trials, pilot feasibility, observational studies, diagnostic accuracy, prediction and preclinical experiments. CONSORT/SPIRIT, STROBE, STARD, TRIPOD+AI and ARRIVE are reporting resources; they do not replace a calculation matched to the planned analysis.

For pilot planning, begin with the [CONSORT randomized pilot/feasibility extension](https://www.bmj.com/content/355/bmj.i5239) and [Albers and Lakens on uncertain pilot effects](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2017.09.004). The linked NIHR HTA document is an archived example; consult current funding-call requirements for an actual application.

For prerequisite reminders and common questions, use the [glossary](../student-materials/glossary.md) and [common mistakes](../student-materials/common-mistakes.md), also included in the tutorial. R code and formulas remain visible; the added interpretation connects their output with the research question.
